Hi, I’m Ran! I study **Computer Science** and **Modern Culture & Media** at Brown University. I’m interested in how tech, power, and sociolegal understandings of new media co-produce each other. More concretely, I'm interested in critical, sociotechnical, and enfranchising approaches to **machine learning** and **robust cryptographic provenance**.

In the past, I’ve had the luck to RA at Brown’s [DISCO Lab](https://discolab.cs.brown.edu/), where I researched and helped engineer AI-supported systems that help no-code communities design custom moderation tools on federated social media. [^1] I’ve also visualized open-source lobbying data for the Watson School of International and Public Affairs.
[^1]: (How might community-based moderation demand different algorithmic affordances than top-down, centralized moderation? How can we use small AI thoughtfully?)

Right now, I’m spending my waking hours working on my senior thesis, which approaches AI-related intellectual property concerns through a sociotechnical and ML + cryptography lens:
 - **[HCI]** Across creative mediums, how do artists’ **sociolegal contexts** shape the types of individual and collective action that are available to them to **refuse AI scraping**?[^2] 
 [^2]: At the moment, I’m particularly interested in [bottom-up refusal as an act of design](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3630107), and the discrepancy between the imagined affordances of a resistive tool and its actual algorithmic affordances.
 - **[CRYPTO / ML]** Further up the regulatory pipeline, I’m interested in how techniques from machine learning and cryptography can help the public quickly and verifiably audit tech companies’ compliance to data protection regulations. My thesis explores ways to **harden commitment-based non-membership proofs against adversarial augmentation**–such that these non-membership proofs can be used more robustly to guarantee that private data hasn’t been used in AI training datasets.

I’d really love to talk about any of this (new ideas and pushback are both so valuable!), which I suppose means some sort of online deliverable that more helpfully sparks conversation - please be patient for this :)

More broadly in life, some things I love are [visual art](/art) (comic art, printmaking), the critical history of language technology, and all of Kaveh Akbar’s Pilgrim Bell poems.
